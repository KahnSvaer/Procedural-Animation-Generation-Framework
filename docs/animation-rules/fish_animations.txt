================================================================================
Rules for Fish Animation (Research Notes)
================================================================================

General Philosophy
------------------
The goal is not to perfectly reproduce biological swimming, but to generate
plausible procedural animations using morphology-derived rules. Most rules
should be inferred from the segmented mesh rather than hardcoded per species.


================================================================================
1. Pectoral (Side) Fins
================================================================================

Two broad functional classes appear to exist.

Type A - Flexible Maneuvering Fins
----------------------------------
Examples:
- Goldfish
- Angelfish
- Reef fish

Characteristics:
- High mobility.
- Used for hovering, braking and turning.
- Frequently perform rowing or figure-eight motions during slow swimming.
- Fold close to the body during burst swimming.

Animation Rules:
- Large motion amplitude.
- High flexibility.
- Independent left/right motion.
- Fold towards body as swimming speed increases.


Type B - Hydrofoil Fins
-----------------------
Examples:
- Sharks
- Tuna
- Dolphins
- Whales

Characteristics:
- Considerably stiffer.
- Primarily provide lift and directional control.
- Smaller range of motion.
- Behave similarly to hydrofoils.

Animation Rules:
- Small oscillation amplitude.
- Mostly rigid rotation.
- Motion follows body orientation.
- Primarily change body orientation rather than generate thrust.

Possible Detection:
- Large span relative to body.
- High aspect ratio.
- Large horizontal extension from body.


================================================================================
2. Pelvic Fins
================================================================================

Current Idea:
- Mostly passive.
- Skinning may be sufficient.

Possible Improvements:
- Add slight delayed motion.
- Add simple drag/inertia model.
- Follow nearby body motion rather than being actively animated.


================================================================================
3. Dorsal and Anal Fins
================================================================================

Current Idea:
- Mostly passive appendages.

Possible Improvements:
- Simulate inertial lag.
- Small damped oscillations.
- Limited deformation according to fin stiffness.

Research Question:
- Can fin stiffness be estimated from aspect ratio or thickness?


================================================================================
4. Tail
================================================================================

Primary source of propulsion.

Two primary motion types:

Horizontal Oscillation
----------------------
Examples:
- Most fishes
- Sharks
- Goldfish
- Tuna

Body bends left-right.


Vertical Oscillation
--------------------
Examples:
- Whales
- Dolphins

Body bends up-down.

Possible Detection:
- Compare tail orientation with pectoral fin orientation.
- Estimate dominant oscillation plane from morphology.

Research Notes:
- Additional horizontal bone chain may be useful for whale-like tails.
- Investigate whether tail base width relative to tail span correlates with
  stiffness or oscillation amplitude.


================================================================================
5. Body Motion
================================================================================

Body flexibility varies significantly.

Possible categories:

- Rigid
- Semi-rigid
- Flexible
- Highly flexible

Examples:
- Tuna -> mostly rigid
- Goldfish -> moderate flexibility
- Eel -> highly flexible

Animation Parameters:
- Wave amplitude.
- Wave frequency.
- Wave propagation speed.
- Wave damping.


================================================================================
6. General Morphological Properties
================================================================================

Each segmented part should ideally have procedural parameters.

Possible parameters:

- Stiffness
- Root stiffness
- Tip stiffness
- Motion amplitude
- Oscillation frequency
- Motion plane
- Rest pose
- Passive / Active
- Symmetry
- Phase offset
- Maximum bending angle
- Inertial lag
- Damping


================================================================================
7. Speed Dependent Behaviour
================================================================================

Low Speed
---------
- Larger pectoral fin movement.
- Smaller body oscillation.
- More steering corrections.

Medium Speed
------------
- Moderate body wave.
- Reduced pectoral motion.

High Speed
----------
- Pectoral fins fold.
- Larger tail oscillation.
- Stronger body wave.
- Reduced unnecessary appendage motion.


================================================================================
8. Future Research Questions
================================================================================

- Can fin stiffness be estimated directly from geometry?
- Does fin aspect ratio predict flexibility?
- Can tail shape predict swimming style?
- Can body thickness predict wave propagation?
- Can local PCA descriptors be used to estimate fin stiffness?
- Can skeleton topology alone determine appropriate animation rules?
- Which parameters can be inferred automatically from segmentation?
